# Seven Virtues of Rule
Emperor Xin brought these ideals along with the Lissalan faith from Azlant, using them to form the basis of wise governance in the ancient Thassalonian Empire. Over time, the Runelords abandoned all pretense of noble mindset and openly embraced the seven mortal sins instead.

#### LISSALA
##### *The Order of Virtue*, *The Sihedron Scion*
**Edicts** Gather and share knowledge freely, honor your duty when your word is given, respect and carry out the law
**Anathema** Conceal and keep information secret, break an oath, subvert authority
**Areas of Concern** Duty, fate, rewards for service, runes
**Religious Symbol** Sihedron
**Sacred Animal** Snake
**Sacred Color(s)** Gold and green

Lissala was minor power in the Azlanti pantheon, but her popularity in the Thassalonian Empire focused on a corrupted version of her teaching, giving rise to a splinter cult of the Lissalan faith which ultimately overtook its Azlanti counterpart. Eventually the (original) Azlanti sect was declared heretical by the more numerous adherent across the ocean and it was all but eradicated by the time of Earthfall.

#### Malistokian Chain
A heretical theory which claims the faithful can alter the deity they worship (alt. corrupt) by following the letter of its edicts and anathema but not the spirit. According to the claim. this is how the Thassalonian cult of Lissala seized control of their goddess’s dogma, using a 51% attack style, where high-level clergy gain sway over a majority of the worshippers, and use their influence to gradually move their beliefs.

The idea that gods are subject to the dictates of their faithful at all is an idea that shatters the *ladder of heaven*, a lodestone concept cited in the Windsong Testaments. The *ladder* refers to the hierarchy of gods and mortals, which gives dominion to those higher on the ladder to those below them, vouching safe the divine right of kings, and at the same time giving hope to who despite all their prowess find themselves in need of rescue by a higher power.

“Malistokian Chain” is a provocation meant to conjure images of mortals clasping gods in bondage and stir rebellion and revulsion to there heresy. The heretics themselves do not use this term. In their own words, “Athar”—*belief shapes reality*—is just one of the theories by which these defiers interrogate the mysteries of the great realms beyond the Universe.
