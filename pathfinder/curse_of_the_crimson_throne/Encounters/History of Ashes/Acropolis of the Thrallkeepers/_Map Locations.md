#### ACROPOLIS FEATURES
**Light** Unless otherwise noted, the chambers below the acropolis aren’t lit and exist in total darkness.

**Walls** Beneath the dust, the walls are composed of black granite flecked with blood-red deposits of crystal. With a DC 24 Religion check, intricate rune patterns decorating the walls at regular intervals are revealed to be Thassilonian writing, extolling the virtues of Runelord Karzoug and the power of the goddess Lissala. The ubiquitous seven-pointed Sihedron is also present at regular intervals.

**Doors** Each stone door bears a bas relief of the Sihedron on its face as well. If the symbol is pressed, it clicks and the door slowly grinds up into the ceiling the following round, granting access beyond. The door descends 1 minute later, with a DC 29 Reflex save to avoid taking 3d6 damage and being pinned (restrained). The doors can be jammed open with a (secret) DC 24 Thievery check

**Diplomacy DC 31** or **Intimidation DC 27** to convince the Sklar Quah to assist against the Red Mantis

#### A CROWDED DUNGEON
**Red Mantis Assassins** Four assassins have paid the Cinderlander to help them track the PCs. The assassins enter the acropolis 10 minutes after the PCs. Their primary job is to engage the PCs so that Cinnabar’s ally, the cleric Koriantu, who is scrying on one of the assassins from the safety of her Korvosa hideout, can observe the PCs in combat.

**Sklar Quah** Eats What He Kills and three thundercallers arrive about an hour before the PCs. They proceed quickly to area A3 to hide and observe. Krojun is curious in his own way as to what took place between the PCs and the Skoan Quah at the Bone Council Fire. He is hoping to be discovered and accompany the tshamek for a short time. They claim to be here for entertainment, and to ensure they properly respect the ruin and do not somehow fake their quest to gain the Spherewalker’s Mark.

They do not aid the PCs in a fight against the Red Mantis unless one of the assassins harm them first. The PCs can persuade them to join the fight before such an event with a successful DC 31 Diplomacy check or a DC 27 Intimidation check. If they use intimidation, Eats What He Kills and will speak ill of the PCs (see [[Earning Respect]]).

The Sklar Quah will automatically step in to aid the PCs in any fight against the havero.

Eats What He Kills wants to attain the Spherewalker’s Mark, but his companions grow increasingly nervous about the strange assassins and monsters encountered within the ruin and will force him to leave before then.

#### A2. POOL OF THE HAVERO

A bridge crosses the midpoint of the cathedral, connecting the north and south gallery walks.

**Search** Anyone conducting a thorough search notices the floor is a pool of still water. The pool is cold and stagnant, clogged with silt and a thick layer of dark algae.

**Track** DC 27 Survival to follow the trail of the Sklar Quah through the door into area A3.

If the havero wakes, the Red Mantis watch quietly, entering the fray only if they are noticed or to strike while the PCs are wounded as soon as the tentacles withdraw. If there are no PCs here when the assassins arrive, they avoid the pool, spreading out to investigate the surrounding rooms in numerical order.

#### A4. SHRINE TO KURSHU
>In a curved alcove, a brilliantly colored bas-relief carving depicts a woman with six wings and a serpentine lower body. The coils of her body wind in and out and around a large seven-pointed star. Tiny shards of gemstones embedded in her tail make her scales glisten as though with moisture, but in more places, chunks of the carving have cracked or crumbled away, giving the depiction a strange but undeniable look of age and decay.

**Investigate** DC 25 Religion to determine this room was once a shrine dedicated to Lissala’s herald, Kurshu; known during the Thassilonian period as “Kurshu the Divine Spirit”, the herald is known today as “Kurshu the Undying”, as she lingers despite the fact that her god seems to have moved on. The Thrallkeepers called upon Kurshu during the construction of the acropolis, and this shrine was built to honor her aid. No prayers or offerings have been made here more than a thousand years.

**Treasure** The sapphire and emerald slivers in the statue’s tail collectively total 182 gp in value.

**Development** If the Sklar Quah accompany the PCs into this room, the thundercallers cry and yell in awe and appreicate at the depiction of Kirshu (+3 NP).

#### A5. THE ILLUMACORE
>The floor of this otherwise empty chamber contains a hole in the center, surrounded by a ring of tangled runes. Inside the hole, a shaft filled with brilliant emerald light drops into the depths.

**Investigate** DC 29 Arcana to deduce a traveler who steps into the ring receives the benefit of a *gentle landing* spell, allowing them to float down the shaft of light for 70 feet into area A9. Once there, a traveler who crosses the matching ring of runes on the floor is affected by a *levitate* spell, allowing them to safely ascend back to this room. Both spells end any time a traveler steps out of the shaft.

**Detect Magic** The light is a magical effect as are the ring of runes.

**Red Mantis** If a pair of assassins move through the double doors into this room in search of the PCs, they spend 5 minutes investigating the illumacore before determining what the device is for, at which point they descend to area A9.

**Sklar Quah** The Sklar Quah don’t know what to make of the light-filled shaft. They will not follow the PC’s trail, if it leads this way. However, if Eats What He Kills is accompanying the PCs to the this room, he may be the first to enter the illumacore, rather than show any sign of fear.

#### A6. SHRINE TO LISSALA
>A strange bronze statue stands in the center of the room. The statue depicts a half-snake, half-human creature with the lower body of a serpent coiled about a stand and the upper torso of a slender human woman. Her hands are crossed over her chest to clutch two objects at her shoulders—the right hand holds a large quill, while the left holds a jade-handled whip. Six birdlike wings emerge from the torso’s shoulders, and instead of a head, it has a disk bearing a seven-pointed star. Jade runes run down the statue’s belly and along the length of its snaky lower body.

**Search** DC 26 Perception to notice a drawer at the base of the statue.

**Investigate** DC 25 Religion check to recall that this statue depicts Lissala, the forgotten goddess of runes and fate. The quill may be symbolic of her edict to Gather and share knowledge freely. The whip may be symbolic of her anathema against subverting authority.

**Investigate** The statue’s base contains a wide but shallow drawer. Inside is a 4-foot-diameter circular frame containing numerous wedge-shaped metal plates, each punched with its own intricate design. Each plate slides over or under the two adjacent plates along the outer ring. The ring fits perfectly over the entrance to the illumacore in area A5, projecting a map of what the acropolis once looked like during the height of the Thassalonian empire onto the ceiling. A successful **DC 26 Thievery** check aligns the plates correctly around the illumacore.

**Detect Magic** The whip is magical.

**Treasure** The jade-handled whip can be removed with a little work. It is, however, cursed. See [[Lissala's Whip]].

#### A7. ETERNAL GLYPHS
>The walls of this chamber are decorated with six life-sized bas-relief carvings that depict a diverse collection of priests, each adorned in billowing robes covered in Thassilonian runes. An intricate scripture winds around the carvings, coiling across them a long the walls like an immensely long tangle of ribbons. Several ancient skeletons, some clad in hide armor or clutching Shoanti weapons, lie scattered around the statue’s base.

**Investigate** DC 31 Religion to identify the priests depicted as high-ranking clergy of Lissala. They are engaged thaumaturgical rituals of summoning. These may be the “Thrallkeepers”.

**Detect Magic** The lettering composing most of the scripture is magical.

**Investigate/Trap** The tiny coded lettering on the walls can be deciphered by anyone who understands Thassilonian and succeeds at a DC 31 Religion or Arcana check.
```pf2e-stats
# Eternal Glyphs
## Hazard 9

---
==magical== ==trap==
**Stealth** DC 30 (expert)
**Description** This coded Thassalonian scripture makes dire warnings about life-threatening perils in the acropolis beyond. Anyone who comprehends the warnings is compelled to refrain from any other activity until they have finished reading and made sense of the entire text (characters who do not not understand the writing cannot be affected by this trap). This task is impossible, since portions of the text that have already been studied change and alter themselves slightly to create different nonsense meanings that nonetheless seem incredibly important while they are being studied.

---
**Disable** DC 30 Thievery (master) to destroy the correct carvings or dispel magic (DC 25 counteract, 5th) to deactivate the script's magic.

**Suggested Reading** `[reaction]` (enchantment, incapacitation, mental) **Trigger** A creature deciphers the text, then attempts to stop reading it; **Effect** A powerful suggestion spell grips the reader, who must attempt a DC 25 Will save to stop reading it.
	**Critical Success** The reader is unaffected
	**Success** The reader becomes entranced, increasing their stupefied level by 1. While stupefied, they may take no actions other than reading the scripture for 1 round. After which, the reader may attempt another Will save.
	**Failure** As success, but the effect lasts for 1 hour.
	**Critical Failure** As failure, but the effect last for 1 day.
```

> **Player** Can I decipher the script?

> **GM** (*rolls dice*) Yes, it takes approximately 5 minutes. The scripture is written in some kind of coded Thassalonian. You’re almost done but the writing is denser than anticipated, so deciphering requires an additional 15 minutes of work. The warnings may actually be promises of spiritual reward…this may require more like 6 or 7 hours. Do you continue?
> 
> **Player** What?? Geez, I guess we’ll come back to this some other time. Let’s move on…
> 
> **GM** You try to tear yourself away from the scripture, but you find that… hard to do. Give me a Will save!

#### A11. GOLARION GLOBE
>The walls, ceiling, and floor of this chamber have been painted to resemble a starry sky, giving the illusion of walking through space. A ten-foot-diameter stone sphere floats five feet above the floor near the room’s southern wall. Three short metal rods protrude from the lower hemisphere, radiating outward like a tripod, only the rods rest on empty air rather than a solid surface. The sphere appears to have been carefully carved with tiny rivers, mountains, oceans, and forests.

**Investigate** DC 26 Nature to recognize the globe represents the world of Golarion as it appeared in a past age (10,000 years ago to be more precise). There are many features on the globe that seem inaccurate—Varisia, for example, is landlocked except for a narrow southern coastline. Other features are missing as well, such as the Inner Sea, while in some places islands or entire continents exist where none appear today. Further casting the globe’s accuracy into suspicion are the vast swaths of blank surface in some regions, where only vague outlines of continents and oceans appear.

**Detect Magic** The globe is magical. Also, the tripod holding the globe in mid air is magical.

**Investigate** DC 31 Arcana while studying the globe to deduce its function, including the method of exiting the globe once a character has entered it. The globe is hollow—a one-foot-thick shell of stone surrounding a spherical chamber containing magical space that the Thrallkeepers used to cast their minds out beyond Golarion to explore and meditate. Anyone who touches the globe and concentrates on the stars or sky is immediately teleported inside the globe and palced into a state of suspended animation as their mind is cast out into the distance to see strange and alien worlds and float in the void between the stars.

Once, the globe allowed the Thrallkeepers to seek out strange new creatures and attempt to call them into the acropolis, but over the years, the magic of the globe has faltered. Today it does little more than provide a strange series of visions to the user that, over prolonged exposure, can traumatize minds. Every minute a character remains inside, increase their stupefied value by 2 (basic Will save, DC 24). When a the stupefied value reaches 5 or more, the affected character is shunted out of the globe automatically.

**Treasure** The globe is held in place by a triangle of three *immovable rods*. Removing any of the rods is an easy matter—each rod has a button on one end that allows it to be moved—but doing so causes the globe to come crashing down to the floor, with thunderous echoes throughout the ruin (+10 NP), and cracks the globe’s surface. Unless repaired, the globe will cease to function forever in 1d4 weeks.

**Spherewalker’s Mark** A mark manifests on the palm of a character when they emerge from the globe. Bearers of the mark immediately understand its ramifications and understand how to use it.
