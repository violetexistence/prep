#### Sights and Sounds

**Mounds** The mountains flanking the settlements are where it gets its name, not gentle hills by an description. Rather, cave-pocked massifs, the oldest and deepest ossuaries carved straight into the rock above the valley, predating the permanent camp known as Kallow Mounds.

**The Rictus Gate** The northern entrance to the valley is where the most visitors bring their dead. It is named both for the occasion which brings most visitors and the gargantuan skulls that greet them at the gate. The largest belong to landsharks and purple worms. Within the gates are facilities to prepare bodies for their rest (and recycling), guest accommodations, housing for mounts, a few modest dwellings for boneslayers to rest and rejuvenate before heading back out on patrol, and a communal area for the Skoan-Quah to perform group rituals such as a Council Fire.

**The Bone Road** A long and winding processional connects everything. Bones brought to the mounds for burial are walked the length of the Bone Road. Along the way are two and three dwelling family clusters. Where it connects to the clusters and at each end, the road emerges into the light, but the majority of it traverses the distance underground. Every inch is lined with the bones.

**The Cairn of Gaekhen** Topped with a mockingbird carved from wood with dark blue (labradorite) eyes instead of an animal skull. The party recovered the body of Thousand Bones’ natural grandson, whose body was recovered from the Dead Warrens in Act One. Though he was born to Skoan-Quah parents, he chose the Moon Clan after hearing the Song of the Spheres on a night ride across the Cinderlands. During his short time in the camp of the Lyrune-Quah, he was adopted/mentored by one of their Starsingers, a woman named Shimmering Streams. Despite hearing his calling in The Song, Gaekhen struggled to find his place. The Spheres fascinated him, but perilous journeys were something he preferred to sing about instead of undertaking himself. Gaekhen convinced his mentor to let him study in Korvosa over the protests of the elders in her clan. He was studying at the Pantheon of Many, when and angry mob cornered and murdered him in the street. If and when the PCs visit the House of the Moon, if they tell the story of how they recovered one of their clan, she may prove a valuable ally.

**Forgotten Cairn** A pair of mounds with no road leading to them are where the burials for those without family to remember them, those without names, or whose names the Quahs wish to forget. Boneslayers are given the task of walking to these mounds alone with the bodies of the forgotten.

**Yurts** The Skoan-Quah have integrated traditional architecture with modern materials to create uniquely sustainable dwellings, where the clan lives year round. As keepers of the dead for all the clans, the Skoan-Quah are no longer a nomadic people. What they have given up in their survival instincts in the open desert, they have replaced with sustainable engineering and as a side effect, live in relative comfort compared to the other Quahs. Boneslayers still roam the Cinderlands hunting the restless dead, but the majority are here, living their lives, and embarking on drug enhanced vision quests for understanding on the cycle of rebirth and souls. Each dwelling is a living, breathing construct. Steel mesh vanes placed in the eaves form the lungs, capturing wind to  both moderate heat and condense usable water. The outer skin of the ship is made of porous composite layered over insulative bone shingles. Reflective gemstone grit mixed into the composite send solar power down to pumps deep underground forming the beating heart of the ship, which move water and convert solar power into limited steam power throughout the ship. Waste is treated using alchemical components in tanks, forming the bowels which compost and recycle nutrients, filtering through and feeding hanging gardens which live in symbiosis on every level.

**Guest Yurt** This dwelling is more or less self sufficient with at least one trained operator. It can sustain up to 8 guests comfortably along with 4 animal companions. Mounts are nevertheless normally kept in separate accommodations. There are four bedrooms, a large common area, kitchen, and two baths. In addition, a covered garden and outdoor bath is usually accessible depending on the season—which is often setup for outdoor dining as well. Downtime spent here clear is extra regenerative. Clear **2 Stress per day** of downtime spent in the guest yurt.

## Bone Council Fire
In the communal area near the Rictus Gate, this ritual is held once a month or on special occasion (such as now) as needed. By ancient tradition, this is always held outside under the night sky. At the Kallow Mounds there is a permanent fire pit surrounded by cushions where attendees may sit for several hours themselves surrounded in a ring of torches. The Quah’s eldest shaman, Ash Dancer, will burn flayleaf to help attendees relax and open to truth. An [air scamp](https://2e.aonprd.com/Monsters.aspx?ID=2985) works with the shaman to aid those who are open to its effects by directing smoke toward them regardless of which way the wind is blowing.

> **One Life** You have already done my people a great favor by returning the body of one of our warriors (Gaekhen).
>  
> **One Life** I sense now you come to me to ask a favor in return, yet know that by doing this favor, you are helping us all. The Skoan-Quah are a peaceful people, yet we are also all but shunned by our kin. Our willingness to mix with tshamek shames many of my brothers and sisters in the other quahs. Only their respect for our tradition of guarding and protecting the dead of all Shoanti keep them from open hostility against us. My words do not reach their ears when I advise against war on Korvosa. They hear tales of the city in flames, of its king dead, of disease ravaging its people, and they see this as the time foretold. A time when the Shoanti can ride down from these burning lands and reclaim greener lands to the south, lands that were once ours. 
> 
> **One Life** War will not be good for us. My kin do not see that, even crippled, Korvosa remains a powerful enemy. It is best to make your enemies your friends, do you not think? Many of my kin do not see truth in these words. 
> 
> **One Life** They see weakness, they demand action. 
> 
> **One Life** Yet you could not know of the coming war. You come to me with a different favor. Speak of what you wish of me, and perhaps we may find our needs are the same.  
  
**What can you tell us of Midnight’s Teeth?** 
> **Ash Dancer** The Fangs of Kazavon. These are the remains of an ancient enemy. I have only ever heard them mentioned twice and none from the clan of the sun have ever shared more than this, but I can hear them whispered of by protectors long dead and still watching. If any still carry the tale, it is the Sun Shamans.

**How can we talk to the Sun Shamans?**

> **One Life** Sadly, you cannot. You are tshamek. Outlanders. Rightful or not, the Sklar-Quah will see you as the children of those who murdered their ancestors and drove them from the green lands. The Sun Clan does not like outsiders at the best of times. And less so now that war against Korvosa is on every the lips of every Sun Rider.
  
**Then how can we make them see us as allies, not outsiders?** 

> **One Life** You must understand the Sklar-Quah are very certain of these things. They may come to respect lone tshamek after many days of seeing them act honorably, but what you ask is for the Sun Shaman to lay bare the heart of his Quah. The deepest memories of his ancestors. Even if he wanted to, his burn riders would never let you get close to their Sun Shaman. Tshamek have no claim to them. 
> 
> **Thousand Bones** What about Skurak the Reborn? \**smiles enigmatically*\* The Sklar-Quah unbanished a man and welcomed him into their Quah.

**Who is Skurak?**
 
> **Ash Dancer** The legend of Skurak is of a great warrior and greater traitor to the Sklar-Quah. He slew his brother, a rider of even greater courage. To the Sklar-Quah, family is purity…crimes against the family are the greatest a soul can commit. Although Skurak claimed the death was an accident as he and his brother were hunting, others spoke of murder spawned of jealous rage. Skurak was declared a tshamek by the Sun Shaman and cast out, but before he left, he said he would be born again and return to his tribe. 
> 
> **Ash Dancer** This he did. He went to the killing grounds of the great Cindermaw the Clan-Eater. Skurak walked up to the beast carrying only his dagger. Without fear, he dove inside the beast and cut his way out. He returned to the clan and declared he had been reborn, and had left his misdeeds behind in the cleansing fire of Cindermaw’s belly. The legend says the Sun Shaman accepted this and Skurak’s time as a tshamek was spoken of no more.


**What’s a ‘Cindermaw?’**
> **Ash Dancer** A legend… yet one of flesh and blood. Cindermaw dwells on the northernmost edge of Shoanti lands, in the place where Shoanti and orc and giant vie for life and water. His hunting grounds are known as the Feeding Grounds of the Quah-Kael, the land of the Clan Eater. The orcs of Urglin avoid this region; the giants of the north hunt only on its edges. Within, few have returned to tell tales, but those who do speak of a mountain that crawls and of fire that roars.


**Are you saying that we could earn the respect of the Sklar-Quah by being eaten by Cindermaw and then escaping?**  

> **One Life** Perhaps, yet this route is more complex than Thousand Bones suspects. The Sklar-Quah would never believe the words of tshamek on such a matter, nor would they risk travel to the Feeding Grounds just to see foolish outlanders attempt to recreate legends. We could go with you, yet we are Skoan-Quah. Our words would hold little light with the Sun Shaman, I fear.
> 
> **Ash Dancer** They could bring a Truthspeaker.” 

**What is a Truthspeaker?**

> **Ash Dancer** There are those among our people who, after living lives without lie and never speaking falsehood, have earned the title of Truthspeaker. It is a rare honor, one that requires chastity, self-control, and much introspection. There are Truthspeakers among the Sklar-Quah, yet that does not help you. Would that the Skoan-Quah had one. Yet the Skoan-Quah and the Sklar-Quah are not the only Shoanti in the Cinderlands. The Lyrune-Quah, who dwell in the shadow of the Wyvern Mountains, have Truthspeakers among them. If you could perform the ritual of rebirth at the Feeding Grounds of the Quah-Kael in the presence of a Truthspeaker, his words would be all the proof you need to make your claim.
  
**Where would we find the Lyrune-Quah?**  

>**One Life** The Moon Clan are nomads. This time of year, they gather at a place sacred to them, a place called the House of the Moon at the northeastern most edge of the Wyvern Mountains. Yet, be warned. The Lyrune-Quah are unusual among the Shoanti. They do not seek enlightenment through our ancestors, but from the Song of the Spheres. I fear that they will distrust you as tshamek as well, unless they also find your needs among the words of their sacred Song.
